Business Overview

AT&T Inc. embodies a telecommunications titan that provides mobile wireless data, high-speed fiber and broadband internet, as well as IoT solutions and VoIP phone systems for corporate networks. The company is supported by over 100 million individual subscribers, alongside businesses and other entities at the domestic level and over 240 million subscribers total at the international level. The company also holds a substantial presence in brick and mortar locations, offering digital hardware such as cell phones, wearables, wifi modems, and other accessories. With roaming coverage in more than 210 destinations worldwide, AT&T offers compatibility for tourists and frequent travelers to stay online through a daily fee, allowing for talk, text, and internet data allowances. The company has undergone notable transformations over the past decade, including the divestiture of its media assets and a renewed focus on telecommunications. However, performance has remained streamlined and strong, with emergence into a more unified enterprise centered on wireless connectivity, fiber optic internet, and next- and future-generation network infrastructure adaptability. AT&T’s core business model is built around two major revenue engines. First, nationwide 5G wireless coverage allows connectivity to consumers and businesses across the continental United States and beyond. Second, fiber optic data plans are coming to fruition as AT&T’s key focus, allowing demands for data, cloud computing, AI, streaming, and high digital traffic work to be met, with AT&T benefitting as a given. As such, the company acts as a foundational provider in the digital economy, and capitalizes off name brand presence. With the necessity of reliable communications infrastructure, AT&T serves as a traditional utility provider, such as gas, water, and electricity, because of the ubiquitous, almost omnipresent nature of the internet in the present day. Insightfully, AT&T also captures most profits through monthly or yearly subscriptions, meaning the company can yield tangible profits more consistently than other business models, and reinvest these gains back into the company, concurrently supporting dividends to shareholders, reducing debt, and improving network capability. Further, the amount of consumers using an AT&T plan is easier to translate into a dollar amount compared to other platforms with a diverse price range of offered products, allowing for more peace of mind when deciphering current revenue and growth expectations. As AT&T continues building and expanding upon its fiber presence alongside other initiatives like bundling data plans, improving pricing and customer retention, and driving subscriber numbers, future expectations portend growth and reinforced status for AT&T as a powerful, strategic, and adaptable player in both the telecommunications and global industry.

Investment Thesis

  • AT&T occupies a unique, and alluring, position within the equity market. By offering investors a combination of stable, transparent cash flow generation, a track record of compounding yearly returns, sizable growth prospects and future expansion opportunities, shareholder returns, and a strong defensive ballast due to widespread influence both nationally and internationally, AT&T argues itself as an income-oriented, lower-risk buy: perfect for investors seeking a smooth ride of returns over volatility spikes. As one of the largest telecommunications providers in North America, and the biggest in terms of market cap, AT&T’s name presence also provides further credibility.
  • An optimistic harbinger for AT&T’s is the company’s ability to successfully adapt to demand and capture growth, while simultaneously concentrating its business profile while keeping multiple diverse revenue streams and lowering beta and drawdown risk. No longer a broad media conglomerate, AT&T is investing heavily to weaponize demand for fiber and broadband internet into substantial capital gain. By 2030 alone, AT&T has set a goal of reaching more than 60 million fiber locations, doubling current availability of 30 million in over 20 states. This managerial emphasis on fiber highlights the company’s confidence in this market opportunity and, additionally, it is occurring on top of a well-established network infrastructure, which provides wireless data to millions worldwide. These operations are high-growth opportunities, showcasing AT&T’s desire to increase profits in a uniquely low-risk manner. Since inception, and backed by recent yearly financial reports, AT&T continues to add millions of customers, highlighting dedication not just to uphold loyal customers, but also to build relationships with new ones, simultaneously amplifying revenue year over year thanks to the subscription-based business model.
  • AT&T also has promoted a convergence plan, where customers can bundle subscriptions to both wireless and fiber services. This allows a significant proportion of customers to maximize their data and internet needs, which in turn boosts AT&T’s revenue base and income growth. Specifically, fiber networks offer faster speeds, lower latency, and greater scalability than traditional infrastructure utilizing cable or copper systems.
  • For investors focused on income, AT&T remains a logical and attractive choice. As a major, long-term constituent of the S&P 500, AT&T has demonstrated a commitment to investors as a revered pick for defensive portfolios through consistent free cash flow generation, a commitment to sustaining a steady dividend yield, and pursuance of share repurchase opportunities. While mostly considered a value stock, AT&T has also experienced periods of large growth rates, with returns over 40% in 2019 and 2024, adding to upside for investors. AT&T is unlikely to rival the growth of a leading mega-cap tech name, but remains a diamond in the rough in the telecommunications market, outpacing many competitors in terms of growth and commitment to safe dividend yield.

Valuation

At the time of writing, AT&T’s validation appears promising. Boasting a current trailing P/E ratio of 7.47, AT&T surpasses Verizon and T-Mobile, its most immediate competitors (Trailing P/E of 11.17 and 19.07, respectively). This suggests the current share price is relatively inexpensive, and a legible sign for undervaluation. AT&T’s forward P/E rises marginally to 9.83, which is edged out by Verizon’s 9.23 forward P/E, but still outshines T-Mobile’s 17.09. While low chances of unforeseen immense revenue growth potentially keep share price low, AT&T demonstrates that, for the long-term investor, the stock leans towards undervaluation, and is likely a solid pick to ride future waves of company expansion, and hence washing ashore with increased returns. Impressively, recent performance has surpassed investor expectations, notably in revenue and EPS as a result of subscriber growth, aforementioned fiber expansion, and robust free cash flow generation, which bolsters assumptions of undervaluation. As an established name in a mature telecommunications industry, AT&T faces a debt burden that would be negligent to overlook. As one of the most indebted companies in the world, debt remains an Achilles heel to AT&T’s overall performance and valuation. While the company has increasingly reduced its leverage since the unwinding of its core media acquisitions, debt still poses consequences to AT&T’s valuation. With leverage remaining elevated compared to some peers, AT&T faces some valuation slippage, but remains strong in the face of this due to tenacious performance in other areas of the business. The company expects leverage to trend towards long-term targets in coming years, allowing for valuation fears to subside, and further supporting investors interested in ownership. My DCF model indicates that AT&T is undervalued, which is atypical of companies in stable industries like telecommunications. Bull assumptions portend lucrative returns. Given AT&T’s commitment to expansion and debt improvement, high dividend yield, prominence in the industry, low volatility compared to the broader market, and an estimated undervaluation, the company’s stock is an honest, alluring choice, with opportunities for monitoring results of transparent subscriber and earnings reports, therefore making it a reasonable, justified buy for long-term investors.

Risks

Despite being a consistently profitable, relatively low-risk company, it is certainly poignant to address some challenges to AT&T. First, competition is arduous throughout the telecommunications industry. Peer companies such as Verizon and T-Mobile also aggressively pursue subscriber growth through pricing initiatives (which often directly attempt to outcompete each other), promotions, and expansion investments. If pricing differences for customers continuously narrows more between these monarchs of the industry, AT&T could face compressed margins and limited growth in earnings. Second, AT&T’s debt is a major setback to the company’s finances at large, as aforementioned. Resulting from years of acquisitions and capital-intensive infrastructure investments in periods where the company was pursuing media conglomeration, AT&T piled up enormous quantities of debt, which the company now has to dig itself out of. While efforts to improve leverage have been successful, they are still in their relative infancy. Thus, elevated interest rates or setbacks to operations could infringe upon these endeavors. Third, AT&T’s fiber deployment investments are not without risk. The company does foresee long-term profitability and growth in the wake of successful implementation, but it is also hedging its bets heavily into this expansion. If customer adoption slows or competition intensifies from cable providers or regional broadband providers, AT&T will likely be forced to take a loss. Further, even if AT&T does see some immediate success in fiber, if the target earnings goal is not met then growth could slow, meaning revenue gained would not offset expenditures, potentially leading to further setbacks and debt fears. Fourth, the telecommunications industry is heavily regulated. Changes in licensing, consumer protections, broadband policy, or privacy requirements could portend significant slowdown of free cash flow. Last, with the current news of the IPO, SpaceX could emerge as an interesting challenger to AT&T. SpaceX’s Starlink satellite network indirectly trading on the market could threaten to disrupt AT&T’s core business model. Starlink allows for high-speed internet and mobile services that eliminate traditional cell service dead zones due to gaps in tower coverage or network traffic, thus capturing customers in urban and rural settings. With an expanding subscriber base, as well as a massive premium valuation currently, SpaceX could directly contribute to some interesting volatility in AT&T’s stock, which counteracts AT&T’s reputation as a stable value stock.

Conclusion

AT&T is a markedly different company today compared to several years ago. Following the divestiture of its media assets and a renewed business model of providing core telecommunications services, the company has successfully repositioned the company around two key growth drivers: providing 5G wireless data and fiber internet to its main subscriber base in the United States, as well as expanding territory across the globe. Recent company performance, supported by years of prominence, demonstrate that these major strategy shifts are effective and help to position the company for long-term relevance and success. AT&T continues to build and prioritize subscriber growth, leverage the fiber demand, and improve free cash flow in ambitious, but still relatively low-risk managerial methods. Supporting the low-risk argument, AT&T is appealing as a strong value stock due to undervaluation, a respectable CAGR, a low beta demonstrating lower volatility and drawdown chances, and a strong dividend yield. Therefore, it is evident that AT&T plans to benefit its investors, and thus the company itself, through its operational history and new advancements. Peering over the horizon, AT&T is well-placed for success, with performance-driven upside projections whilst simultaneously carrying significantly lower risk than other large-cap stocks. While AT&T does face some risk from competitors, as well as large amounts of debt, with continued improvements to outperform competition and lower leverage, AT&T is holding an optimistic outlook for the future, an outlook that many buy-and-hold investors might find alluring to be a part of. The stock may not deliver the historically explosive upside and returns of emerging companies in other markets, but AT&T’s low risk profile more than makes up for this, instead intending to promise an opportunity for income, stability, and robust long-term appreciation. As such, AT&T reinforces itself as an attractive buy within telecommunications and large-scale businesses. With a dependable business model and emphasis on growth, AT&T justifies a Buy rating at present and in the coming years.
