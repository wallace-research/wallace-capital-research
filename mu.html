Business Overview

Micron Technology, Inc. is a semiconductor manufacturer and global forerunner in memory and storage solutions. Founded and headquartered in Boise, Idaho in 1978, Micron designs, manufactures, and supplies High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), dynamic random-access memory (DRAM), NAND flash memory, and solid-state storage products, all of which play an integral role in the functionality and efficiency of a wide and growing array of electronics. Additionally, Micron has the unique characteristic of being one of three major companies providing the foundational HBM and DRAM supply chain to a plethora of markets, with the other two being South Korean chaebols Samsung and SK Hynix. This rare capability, especially as the standalone supplier located in the Western world, has logically created tremendous growth for Micron both operationally and economically. Consumers of Micron products include a myriad of companies in automotive, computer and smartphone, wireless and telecommunications, other consumer electronics, and other tech industries. Micron also serves a crucial role in data center infrastructure and various industrial applications. Micron focuses the majority of the business around memory and solid-state storage supply directly to computing, artificial intelligence, and GPU system, data center hardware, personal electronic, and automotive systems. Micron products are utilized by leading producers in these fields, including NVIDIA, AMD, Intel in the computing, GPU, and artificial intelligence realm, AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud in the server and storage realm, Apple, Samsung, and Google in the personal electronics realm, and General Motors and Ford in the automotive realm. Evidently, these products are needed by some of the biggest names in the market, demonstrating Micron’s functionality as the backbone of modern technology, and allow for increasingly sophisticated applications in the future. These endeavors will demand even more processing, storage, and energy needs, thus highlighting the indispensable demand for Micron’s products, which have grown to become a component of nearly every computer system. Micron operates using a vertically integrated business model, designing and manufacturing most of its products within company-owned factories. Doing so allows greater control over various deliverable outcomes such as quality, efficiency, and innovation, all while staying within the Micron brand. Continuously investing in research and development has allowed Micron to hold its lofty position. Although the memory semiconductor industry carries risk, often stemming from cyclical trends such as fluctuations in supply (which is often backlogged) and demand, Micron emphasizes advancements in streamlining production, efficiency, and pricing, which are set to play out well given the unrelenting market for ever-faster computing speeds and extensive data storage. Ultimately, Micron’s performance and balance sheet speaks for itself, and current valuation illuminates signs for further upside, thus cementing Micron as a lustrous pick for growth-focused investors.

Investment Thesis

  • Micron has a cogent story for continuous growth within the already booming semiconductor market. This industry serves as the building blocks for modern electronics, including AI, automotive, and healthcare equipment. As outlined in the Business Overview, by supplying one of the few available streams of HBM and DRAM memory to fuel a core subtype of the semiconductor manufacturing market, Micron captures a bottleneck that will likely be incredibly arduous to outcompete due to its cemented position within the sector itself as well as its client base including some of the largest companies on the market. This exceptionally high barrier to entry and concentrated market structure alongside its critical role in modern computing will likely keep Micron’s history of reliability and success at the vanguard in the foreseeable future. While the memory industry is subject to cyclicality, Micron has made devoted efforts to improve supply discipline with the intention of profiting even more from the unprecedented demand AI-focused companies have for Micron products. This fundamentally strengthens the long-term outlook for upside, which makes the company one of the keystone buys for investors interested in computing infrastructure.
  • While Micron’s significant growth catalyst is the rapid expansion of AI, it is worthwhile to mention a few areas in how Micron directly complements it. AI training and inference requires substantially more memory capabilities than traditional computers, which plays into the narrative of the necessity of DRAM and other specialized products. For example, HBM is essential to enterprise AI accelerators and high-performance computing GPUs, enabling faster data transfer speed and improved performance in large-scale systems maintained by massive corporations. As leading tech names continue to pour billions into AI infrastructure and hyperscale data centers, Micron will benefit from the manifold uses for memory demand, an innate component that will also require large investments from these companies. Additionally, despite appreciation in stock price, Micron’s growth story appears to have just begun, as revenue in the last quarter alone skyrocketed past entire years worth of previous earnings, demonstrating robust future potential and upside.
  • Micron looks to profit from multiple growth trends beyond AI and data storage, such as cloud computing, autonomous vehicles and advanced driving assistance systems, industrial automation, and pioneering consumer electronics technologies. As development in these technologies continues to progress, Micron can utilize its core inventory in new revenue streams, which can help to act as a hedge against the cyclical nature of AI demand. A diversified business profile also lucidly draws concentration away from any one component or end market, allowing Micron to potentially shape multiple facets of the future of technology. Unlike other older tech companies whose products eventually grow into obsolescence as innovation advances, every future piece of technology will require greater memory capacity and data-intensive processing, which ultimately sets Micron up for a long-term reign.
  • Recently, Micron has demonstrated a willingness to mirror its operational demand. In times where orders slow, management moderates production in that area. In times where orders peak, production increases to attempt to match, prioritizing efficiency and sustainable output. Interestingly, one of the company’s recent operational challenges occurs not from loss of demand, but rather from too much, where it was unable to fulfill its unexpected order catalog, even while anticipating large growth. If Micron can adapt to fill colossal amounts of customer orders, while still keeping its reputation for top-of-the-line products, free cash flow improvements and expanding margins look promising.
  • Overall, Micron’s leadership in a bottlenecked industry, commitment to innovation, incredibly high demand, disciplined managerial strategies, and exposure to tech inventions, both currently and in the future, place Micron in an alluring position for growth investors. Arguments for Micron revolve around the future outcomes of AI and the integral part Micron will play in manifesting them. Investors looking for exposure to one of the most important segments of the semiconductor industry ought to prioritize Micron and its upside potential as demand for advanced memory products continues to accelerate, thus reconciling the Buy recommendation.

Valuation

Micron’s tremendous returns in the past year have been far from inconspicuous. A core component of the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, the company has driven a substantial part of both indices’ earnings growth and past returns. Surprisingly, this spotlight placed on Micron has not led to a premium valuation, and does not compromise the company’s future perspectives. Micron continues to trade at a discount relative to technology or semiconductor peers. Currently, Micron’s trailing P/E ratio is 21.45. This can be compared to other large names in the pure-play memory as well as the overarching AI space. In the former, key competitors are Korean memory giant SK Hynix and the U.S.-based Western Digital Corporation, with current trailing P/E ratios of 20.69 and 32.93, respectively (SK Hynix’s Korean market listing is used). Regarding the latter, names that are often compared with Micron are Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Broadcom, each with a respective trailing P/E of 172.47 and 64.67. Micron’s current forward P/E is 6.12, a figure relatively similar to close competitor SK Hynix at 7.48, but incredibly lower than all other listed competitors, with Western Digital Corporation at 28.90, AMD at 73.53, and Broadcom at 20.79. Using trailing P/E ratios as a valuation tool highlights that Micron is roughly fairly valued to undervalued, whereas forward P/E demonstrates large undervaluation when held against multiple companies in similar areas. The risks of a valuation trap appear low due to Micron’s continuous growth reports and in-demand products. Something that is almost unseen in companies with such a large market presence is Micron’s PEG ratio of 0.14. While P/E and PEG ratios are certainly promising, it is important to keep in mind, however, that some memory companies can experience this sort of trend due to the cyclical nature of the industry, where an incredible quarter can drive up earnings immensely. While the stock price will rise too, the earnings are simply too great of a denominator for the ratio not to be noticeably small compared to other well-established companies. Nonetheless, this exceptionally low number demonstrates undervaluation, and places Micron as a likely bargain on the market if an investor is willing to take on the stock’s volatility and cyclicality. My DCF model underscores this consensus, also suggesting that Micron is heavily undervalued even with moderate yearly revenue growth assumptions compared to the recent boom. A limitation to Micron’s financials is heavy capital expenditures, which were captured in the model, but this does not drag down free cash flow. The company is in an incredible position for growth, with upside projections illustrating that it will likely be a wise investment for quite some time. While being far from a new company, these growth prospects are backed by the Micron’s relative infancy in being a key shaper of the AI, storage, and tech landscape. For five decades, Micron has survived the volatile nature of the chip industry. By steadily improving its business model, Micron earned its golden opportunity, and shows no signs of slowing down, and therefore encompassing a strong Buy argument.

Risks

Although Micron presents a compelling investment opportunity by many standards, addressing potential risks that could threaten Micron’s high-growth conviction are necessary. First, Micron is embedded within the notoriously cyclical semiconductor industry. Imbalances in manufacturing output or consumer and demand can affect profitability. Periods of excess production and weaker demand have historically led to declines in memory prices, which can strain the revenue and earnings of Micron’s fundamental product portfolio. Second, although there is little competition, the three main players compete fiercely. Samsung and SK Hynix, both of which possess financial, productional, and market share qualities that can potentially rival Micron, are pouring capital into further research and development of their technologies. These financial oligarchs will likely maintain competitiveness for many years, placing pressure on Micron to keep its pace. A slowdown in operational efficiency or product failure could have drastic implications on Micron’s future outlook in the eyes of investors. Third, it is also worthwhile to take Micron’s increased exposure to artificial intelligence infrastructure into account. While AI has become one of the market’s most significant growth drivers, and Micron has refined its business model to capture this upswing, further investments at a global economic scale into hyperscale data centers, cloud infrastructure, and AI hardware are necessary to write Micron’s story for momentous upside. If demand experiences a fundamental halt or change in any of these areas, then Micron will be placed in a precarious situation threatening to upend previous successes. Further, investor expectations are currently elevated; while undervaluation creates some ballast, slowdowns in AI-related spending as a whole will negatively impact Micron, which can often act as a microcosm of the memory and semiconductor manufacturing industry. Conversely, in many ways, the AI narrative depends on Micron’s performance. If Micron has a poor earnings quarter, investor confidence in AI as a whole can falter due to Micron’s share in the market. If doubts are instilled in the back of investors’ minds for too long, then stock price growth can face marked risks. Fourth, geopolitical and trade-related risks due to the wide-spanning network of semiconductor companies across numerous countries are likely unavoidable. Manufacturing, assembly, and end markets are intertwined, with the largest presence in North America, Asia, and Europe at present. Any increased trade regulations, scrutiny, tariffs, or tensions between major countries could disrupt these networks, limiting access to vital international markets. This roadblock would likely increase production costs and create complex managerial conundrums. Micron especially would suffer as the company generates more than half of its revenue from outside the United States. Fifth, future capital expenditures will likely be an ongoing challenge. Due to the intense capital requirements of semiconductor manufacturing, as well as the investments needed to develop new technologies, expand operational capacity, and remain competitive in the field, Micron will need to consistently accelerate its expenses. This will likely not cause much concern if product orders continue to be overwhelming. However, if demand or sentiment weakens, future investments fail to generate expected returns or have negative ROI, or if delays in industrial expansion or production scaling occur, Micron’s free cash flow could take a blow and constrain future initiatives. Last, Micron’s stock is probably not suited for a risk-averse value investor. With historical periods of immense volatility, seen in a beta more than double the S&P 500 benchmark, fluctuations in earnings expectations, memory prices, technology spending, and macroeconomic conditions could shift shareholder value significantly. While this might create strong buying opportunities for growth-oriented investors, or benefit dollar-cost averaging, investors should recognize that Micron does not provide the same level of stability or diversification as more mature or defensive sectors or commodities. Overall, these risks warrant awareness, but should likely not lead most investors away from Micron’s advantages. The company’s leadership, undervaluation, and rooted growth potential create opportunities that are difficult to ignore.

Conclusion

The 21st-century will likely see a few renowned memory tycoons, with Micron being one of these. Serving as a critical supplier of memory and solid-state storage solutions that power modern computing, the company enjoys reaping the benefits of booming AI, cloud computing, data center, and advanced consumer and commercial electronics demand. Combined with a disciplined approach to manufacturing and investment, as well as being on the receipts of some of the world’s largest technology and automotive companies, Micron builds upon its durable competitive position with numerous catalysts for growth. Some risks such as cyclicality and reliance on AI demand exist, but the economic zeitgeist of long-term expansion and development in AI and its infrastructure persists. Refinements to Micron’s operational efficiency, higher-value products, and capital allocation beyond its already elevated position will provide the building blocks for further revenue growth, profitability, and increased free cash flow. It also appears to be a legitimately desirable moment in time to invest, as the company is largely undervalued based on multiple analyses despite surging returns and widespread investor awareness. Therefore, while the stock may experience some significant volatility, Micron’s strategic importance in a high barrier to entry market, powerful business and financial profile, and participation in growth trends likely to develop for many all look to place Micron as a strong buy. For growth- or AI-focused investors, Micron’s pioneering name continues to hold true, embodying a compelling investment with large appreciation potential, thus reinforcing the Buy recommendation currently.
