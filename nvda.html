Business Overview

The self-proclaimed “World Leader in Artificial Intelligence Computing”, NVIDIA is a tech giant revered globally for the design and creation of the GPU in the late 1990s. What initially became synonymous with PC gaming systems has since evolved into something far larger, with NVIDIA’s GPU becoming the backbone of modern accelerated computing, including the current boom in AI developments and demand. Thus, over the past two decades, the company has expanded beyond gaming, now revolving around data centers, AI, advanced computing, robotics, and other cutting edge applications. Currently sitting as the keystone, frontrunning component of both the S&P 500 and NASDAQ, wherever there is a need for powerful, advanced, and increasingly specialized computing, NVIDIA is usually the individual or professional consumer’s first choice. Its CUDA software ecosystem, upgrades to existing GPU developments, networking products, and AI-centered hardware have established a moat that is growing continuously wider. Thus, NVIDIA has become a pivotal provider for the computing and AI revolution taking place.

Investment Thesis

  • NVIDIA remains at the pinnacle of accelerated computing worldwide, carrying this title for years. The company additionally holds a solidified international presence within China and other foreign governments, ensuring American stock droughts will not quell its growth. Yet, it is important to concurrently note that geopolitical tensions may eventually hinder this international exposure. As such, NVIDIA’s strong market presence is likely to be held in the near term, and long-term performance will depend upon further expansions into AI, as well as the demand for AI itself, which does not show any large signs of slowing down. Furthermore, unlike smaller AI or tech startups, NVIDIA is the most obvious choice when it comes to AI investments, meaning the risks that other companies heavily dependent upon AI might hold are not carried to the same extent by NVIDIA. My DCF model projects consistent growth over five years, but poses a risk of overvaluation in later years.
  • With continued expansion into promising tech markets boasting uncapped earning potential, such as automotive, and robotics industries, alongside maintaining existing relationships with cemented enterprises such as Microsoft and Amazon, NVIDIA has solidified itself as a highly stable, yet exponentially profitable company that is also remarkably diversified within the technology sector. The company’s existing scale and track record of success provide a strong flow of profitability, and the aforementioned developments into new areas pave multiple promising avenues for future growth.
  • NVIDIA’s products, especially in the realm of AI computing, are some of the most sought after in contemporary technology. If these industries are expected to grow to new heights, NVIDIA will be present along every step of the way, promising booming capital gains to investors. However, if AI demand stagnates, or other competing corporations present more cost-effective or efficient products, then NVIDIA may suffer. Additionally, emerging advancements in industry such as quantum computing may render NVIDIA’s current output obsolete if they fail to competently adapt. In brief, if an investor is considering buying and holding NVIDIA long term, then that investor ought to monitor the company’s overlap into those sectors, as well as broader trends such as continued AI demand, developments of competitors, and any execution risk facilitated by expansions into new computing markets.

Valuation

NVIDIA trades at an unparalleled P/E ratio when held against peers such as AMD and Intel (Trailing P/E of 33.71 compared to 77.94 and 904.17, respectively), suggesting it is currently undervalued based on both past performance and future expectations (Forward P/E of 20.28 compared to 30.40 and 82.64, respectively). NVIDIA’s PEG ratio sits at 0.60, demonstrating continued resounding undervaluation, and hence growth potential. NVIDIA is expected to have strong future yearly revenue returns, but potentially not as streaky as recent years. Thus, on its surface, NVIDIA appears inexpensive relative to the company’s earnings growth. However, the same streaky earnings seen in the past create a somewhat difficult situation to interpret, especially in the context of valuation and future growth, as even a small change in expectations for NVIDIA’s future can have a sizable effect on a valuation model. My DCF model attempts to account for these factors, and suggests that NVIDIA is prominently undervalued even while assuming more modest, but still booming revenue growth in the future. As a whole, NVIDIA is expected to maintain strong yearly revenue, and it appears evident that share value will continue to increase, though perhaps not as large and rapid as the past, as NVIDIA is already priced to see this success.

Risks

While NVIDIA currently occupies a position that could be widely considered alone at the top of its field, competitors (either pre-existing or in the future) are undoubtedly bound to challenge the company’s position. Already, custom silicon from companies such as Apple is presenting itself as a flea on NVIDIA’s back, as these large technology names have illustrated that specialized chips can be designed internally. Competitors within the semiconductor industry are also continuing to innovate new products meant to directly compete with NVIDIA’s GPU and AI accelerators. Further, aforementioned advancements in technology may throw NVIDIA into a tough position where the corporation may have to adapt to new markets, and potentially losing their independence if they become reliant on other companies to outsource products. In a highly liquid market such as tech, which is expected to continue growing rapidly, slowdowns and stagnant periods are also inevitable, which may pressure NVIDIA’s status and market value. In a subsequent manner, strong investor enthusiasm and speculation in the high-performing, yet nebulous and dynamic tech market may lead to overvaluation. This rhetoric has been demonstrated in the DCF model. Further, due to political tensions between the United States and China, governments both of which NVIDIA is contracted by, the company may face some regulatory challenges and export restrictions, especially in the areas of AI and chip technology.

Conclusion

The risks to NVIDIA’s shareholder value remain prudent at best, and overly cautious at worst, as the company’s expected growth, mirroring the global demand for continuously advancing technology, far outweighs any drawbacks a temporary market slowdown may create. NVIDIA’s key markets remain amongst some of the most versatile industries. Barring a major tech crash, it is difficult to conceive of a scenario where the company will not remain a profitable, grounded presence in the market. In times of strife, fields such as AI are growing in national defense usage. In times of peace, AI is growing in recreational and professional usage. Further, NVIDIA stock remains undervalued, as highlighted by multiple valuation metrics, solidifying that it still deserves to earn a buy recommendation even with its historical hysteria period of rapid appreciation. Even if explosive past returns level off due to large amounts of outstanding shares and a more premium valuation, NVIDIA is still projected to yield consistent, lucrative returns as a result of stable, recurring inflows of revenue. In many ways, NVIDIA is a staple of a modern 21st-century future, which is intended to be shaped by continuous technological advancements. Whichever of these advancements, whether it be AI, autonomous vehicles, or another new computing renaissance, NVIDIA’s ability to adapt and capture a vast proportion of market value demonstrates that the company is an essential part of the next generation.
